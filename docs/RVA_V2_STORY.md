## Premise and core promise

**Pitch:** A temp city worker with a truck and a hi-vis vest learns that every group fighting over Richmond's future is sincerely trying to help, and that each has a number of people it is willing to hurt to do it.

**Theme:** Everyone thinks they are saving Richmond and everyone is willing to destroy part of it to do so. Characters have a sincere Believer wing and an expedient Means wing; the latter's methods gradually compromise the former's goals.

The **Richmond Connector** is a *ghost project*: a proposed east-side / downtown / James River transportation-and-redevelopment link perpetually in planning. Its alignment shifts with funders. Its concrete products are studies, contracts, and strategic real-estate purchases. Do not model it as an imminent, fully funded construction megaproject before the late-game choice.

Mara Bell is a Richmond native who returned home and works on a temporary municipal street-maintenance crew. A **forged night-shift work order** puts her truck near a Church Hill scaffold fire, where it is photographed. A tax lien threatens the Church Hill rowhouse she co-owns with her brother Cal; the house is the holdout parcel on the preferred Connector alignment. Off-the-books work for rival civic factions seems the only way to protect both her job and the house. At mission 15, Mara learns the Connector is a ghost project and that investor **Winslow Tate** manufactured the lien, while his fund genuinely helps other households. Cal sells his share in mission 22. Mara then confronts Tate at a staged groundbreaking and chooses what it means to do good.

### Plot-bearing objects and escalations

- **Night Shift Log:** Truck telematics data proving who was present on the fire night; all sides seek it. At the climax, Mara holds the log.
- **Holdout parcel:** Shared family rowhouse, the only parcel obstructing the preferred alignment. The sibling dispute is a personal conflict, not a generic collectible.
- **Net Good ledger:** The story's antagonist records households helped and harmed; the game tracks equivalent player outcomes.
- **Act escalation:** I gray-area paperwork and sign swaps; II trespass and tampering; III sabotage and blackmail; IV consequences of the player's chosen methods. Keep harm non-lethal and largely offscreen.

**Manchester is contested, not Pod-controlled:** multiple groups depend on its industrial sites, riverbank roads, bridges and rail. **Jackson Ward** is not a faction HQ. Treat its historic interstate displacement seriously, with historian consultation before authored mission details.

Factions coexist in neighborhoods. Do not turn real residential districts into stereotyped enemy-occupied territories.

## The Standing Committee

An old **city council standing committee never formally adjourned**. Members mark their homes with giant plastic skeletons; they enjoy the 'standing' pun. Distributed through the Fan, Church Hill and other older neighborhoods, members include retired clerks, homeowners and institutional old-timers. Their power is narrowly procedural: forgotten easements, unrevoked ordinances, and an obscure veto.

- **Believer purpose:** Keep civic memory intact and the city functioning.
- **Means apparatus:** **The Files**, a drawer of old secrets used as leverage.
- **Story function:** Provides original parcel plat in **mission 21** and can freeze the Connector through a procedure used in **mission 25** / Frozen ending.
- **Foreshadowing:** Skeletons visible in the first hour; seasonal/civic-date costumes; optional pattern-based collectible hunt.
- **Not** the main villain or an all-powerful conspiracy. Tate, Cal and Dell remain the emotional center.

## Geography and progression

Use real Richmond street positions, shapes, terrain and architectural identities; fictional names for agencies, corporations and bridges in narrative content. Verify route/bridge specifics against GIS before authored mission requirements.

- **Handcraft:** Church Hill; a connected Shockoe Bottom/downtown spine.
- **Hero blocks:** Scott's Addition and the Fan.
- **One researched authored sliver:** Jackson Ward ('Before the Highway').
- **Mostly procedural with a few landmarks:** Manchester, Carytown, VCU, Southside; minimal Henrico/Chesterfield edges.
- **Map access:** All geography exists; Act I is mostly north of the James, Act II opens a Southside bridge, and Act III opens the broader network through permit/bridge changes. Avoid invisible walls.
- **Geography as a mechanic:** Church Hill grades affect cargo momentum/brake fade; Fan rowhouses break line of sight; bridges force reroutes; rail crossings cause time constraints; city–county boundary hands off enforcement.
- **World consequences:** Capped authored changes, not a continuously simulated city.

### Endings: one final decision, conditional eligibility

1. **Built:** Tate's alignment goes ahead; Mara takes a cut.
2. **Frozen:** Committee procedure halts Connector and Tate's fund.
3. **Third Alignment:** Smaller public parcel use and modest legitimate aid fund. Requires **low Collateral and Believer contacts**.
4. **The Fixer:** Mara replaces Tate and perpetuates aggressive altruism. Requires **high Collateral and Means contacts**.

Use one final choice and conditional endings/epilogue tone; do **not** author four full Act-IV campaign branches.

## Five anchor mission contracts

- **13 Hill Climb / Scott's Addition → Church Hill:** deliver cast-iron cornice uphill before dawn. Weight, slipping cargo, brake fade, cobbles; re-strap after a break while painting route diversion to hold off a tow truck.
- **20 Freight Delay / Shockoe crossing → Manchester:** a freight train blocks crossings while rivals compete to move contracted cargo; signals, timed crossing and crane. The fanciful train-coupling moment must be made safely playable and non-lethal.
- **17 Bridge Count / downtown river crossings:** convoy vs cargo-bike ride, closing/rerouting bridge routes through Line-Striper segment states.
- **12 Double Booked / Fan block:** overnight survey-marker swap for both Albanico and the Pod, exploiting work-zone credibility; both leaders appear.
- **21 Skeleton Crew / Fan basement:** original plat and procedural vote; giant skeleton disguise and a points-of-order minigame.

## Tone boundaries and open approvals

Dark comedy; slapstick props and vehicles **without firearm combat**. If arson, blackmail or gridlock jeopardize people, harm is consequential, non-lethal and predominantly offscreen; avoid instructional realism. Factions are fictional; avoid stereotyping real neighborhood residents. The Jackson Ward mission needs historical research.

Creator approvals still open in the v2 source: ghost vs real Connector (default ghost); Mara co-owns house (yes); returning native (yes); Committee role (recurring); combat (slapstick only); real geography with fictional institutions (yes); researched Jackson Ward sliver (yes); staged access (yes); Means darkness (non-lethal dark comedy); visible player choice flag for mission-23 house outcome (yes).
