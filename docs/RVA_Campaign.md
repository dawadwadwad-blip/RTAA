# RVA: Aggressive Altruism campaign

Working direction from the November 18 design handoff v2. The [30-mission beat outline](RVA_Mission_Beats.md) carries the story through to the four endings and defines the first Unreal slice. The [architecture manifest](../ARCHITECTURE_MANIFEST.md) describes the relationship to the existing runtime and GIS work.

## Premise

Mara Bell is a returning Richmond native and a temporary municipal street-maintenance worker. She and her brother Cal co-own their late mother's Church Hill rowhouse. A forged overnight work order puts Mara's truck beside a scaffold fire. A tax lien arrives that week. Her house sits on the Connector's preferred route, and her truck's Night Shift Log can establish the vehicle's movements. Mara takes off-the-books faction jobs to buy time.

Winslow Tate engineered the lien and helped keep the Connector perpetually in planning. A moving alignment keeps studies, contracts and land acquisitions flowing. Tate's housing and small-business fund also helps real people in the story. At the midpoint Mara learns both things. Dell admits she helped flag the parcel to divert money toward her crew's lost pensions. Cal eventually sells his own stake to keep his employees afloat. Mara reaches the groundbreaking holding the log and has to decide what she will trade for a result she believes in.

| Character | What they are protecting | Where they cross the line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mara Bell** | The family house, then the people affected by the work she takes | The player chooses when to use leverage and who pays for it. |
| **Cal Bell** | His repair business and its employees | He sells his share of the family house in mission 22. |
| **Dell Okafor** | The maintenance crew and its lost pensions | She skims from contracts and helps flag Mara's parcel. |
| **Winslow Tate** | A housing and small-business fund he can point to with pride | He pressures holdouts with liens and considers his own tally of harm acceptable. |
| **Lenora Pike** | The value of predictable rules | She bends them for people she trusts and later helps Mara get a vote. |
| **The retired clerk** | The city's institutional memory | The clerk lets the Standing Committee use its files for favors and leverage. |

## Campaign structure

| Act | Main missions | Map access | Story movement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I. Work Orders** | 1–6 | North-bank core | A forged order, the fire and the lien push Mara into overtime jobs. |
| **II. Clients** | 7–14 | First Southside crossing opens | Mara meets both wings of the groups that hire her and takes Tate's offer to clear the initial lien. |
| **III. Escalation** | 15–23 | Remaining crossings and county edges open | The ghost project and the fund come into focus. Dell confesses, Cal sells and Mara loses her job and effective control of the house. |
| **IV. Richmond's Future** | 24–30 | Connected world | Pike and an uneasy coalition get Mara to the groundbreaking. One podium choice sets up the final outcome. |

The target is 30 authored main missions over roughly 9–12 hours. Closures and permits explain the staged access in the story. Neighborhood plans use real Richmond geography and fictional agencies, companies and bridge names. Geographic details in a beat stay provisional until the pilot route is checked against the GIS plan.

## Playable factions and territories

Mara can work for a faction's **Believers**, who pursue its stated goal under agreed limits, or its **Means** contacts, who accept collateral costs to get it done. A wing is a contact and a set of job options. It uses the same driving, towing, marking and placement systems as the rest of the campaign.

| Playable group | Believers and Means | Home territory and overlap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Albanico Association** | Review Board and Mortar Circle | Church Hill is the radical base. The Fan is the moderate base and overlaps the Committee. |
| **The Pod** | Prime Builders and the Closers | Scott's Addition flagship. Manchester is contested and hosts the patron's site trailer. |
| **Transit & Cargo-Bike Militia** | Dispatch and the Spoke Cell | VCU and Broad Street. Riverfront trail access brings it into Shockoe Bottom and bridge disputes. |
| **Asphalt & Truck-Nutz Coalition** | Asphalt Samaritans and the Hitch | Southside along Hull Street and Midlothian Turnpike, with influence toward Chippenham and the Chesterfield edge. |
| **Small Independents** | Local owners and the Landlord Club | A client network concentrated in Carytown and Shockoe Bottom. The contacts can disagree with each other. |

**Outside Capital** is Tate's patronage network inside the Pod story. **Compliance**, led by Pike from the downtown municipal district, supplies permit, citation, tow and pursuit pressure. **City–county rivalry** appears as a jurisdiction change. Enforcement drops one tier at the county handoff and the responding behavior changes. The existing civic subsystem still exposes its broader prototype keys for developer testing.

## The Standing Committee

The Standing Committee is a decades-old council standing committee that was never formally adjourned. Retired clerks and homeowners kept its records, and the decoration swaps grew into a mutual-aid network around it. Surviving ordinances and easements give the Committee narrow procedural leverage. Its Means wing, **the Files**, is willing to use old secrets to persuade people. The society has no central faction territory. Its densest cluster is in The Fan, with other homes in Church Hill and historic districts. All member households are authored for the story.

Giant plastic skeletons show up in the first hour. A bare skeleton marks a member's place, a vest can signal a favor and a folding chair can mark a meeting. Other outfits can follow the civic calendar or the owner's taste. The existing **Under the Safety Vest** hand-truck favor gives curious players an early clue and an optional courtyard route. Mission 11 introduces the former clerk on the story path. Mission 21 obtains the original parcel plat and mission 25 earns the procedure that can freeze the Connector. Early favors can make those jobs easier. The story supplies its own required evidence.

## Mechanics and production limits

- **Work Orders and the Method Dial.** Reuse stripe, patch, tow, deliver, swap, block and tail. Use Kind, Pointed and Hard for jobs with room for a real method choice. Author full alternates for about 12 key jobs. A hard method can pay more or be faster and leave a visible bill in Collateral or Compliance.
- **Heavy driving and the Line-Striper.** Use weight, slope, slick streets and grades as routing inputs. Prototype authored road segment changes first. Drivers should react to a marked or closed segment at the next decision point. Expand to real traffic once that delay reads well on one block.
- **Hi-Vis and Compliance.** Show the work-order boundary and the effect of acting outside it. Prototype citation, tow and code enforcement. Add the later police tier and county handoff when pursuit behavior is ready.
- **Net Good, Collateral and Strain.** Reveal the helped and harmed household totals in mission 15. Track Collateral when actions have a confirmed cost and show it before the player commits. Four district Strain states drive a campaign-wide cap of 12–16 authored visual swaps. Favor contacts, heat and the ending flags are small state changes.
- **Hearing and city access.** Reuse one hearing floor map, a records office and the Committee basement. Keep physical bridge or permit gates for staged access. Use real geography to guide routes, with each set piece verified before placement.
- **First slice.** Budget 20–30 minutes for Night Shift, a shortened Hill Climb and one Kind-versus-Hard faction job. Keep a skeleton payoff on the same walkable block. Prove driving and the Method Dial there before expanding. The [Night Shift build card](RVA_Mission_Beats.md#night-shift-first-build) defines the first pass.

### Decisions carried forward as working defaults

The Connector is a ghost project, Mara shares the Church Hill house with Cal, and the Standing Committee is a recurring procedural ally. The current beats also assume returning-native Mara, offscreen non-lethal harm, slapstick vehicle-and-prop combat, staged map access, fictional bridges and an advance warning for a conditional loss in mission 23. The proposed earnest Jackson Ward sequence stays in research until local review. Exact bridge staging and ending thresholds stay tunable.

### Continuity decisions in the beat outline

Tate's Act II bridge payment clears the original lien. Cal later sells only his share. The threat of a forced sale in the non-fire branch comes from the resulting ownership dispute, giving both plot beats a distinct consequence. The Night Shift Log establishes the truck's movements. The dispatch evidence and Dell's account connect that timeline to the forged order. The Standing Committee can stop the project through procedure without replacing Tate as the main antagonist.

## First favor graybox

Enable the local `RVACivic` runtime plugin and place one `StandingCommitteeFavor` actor into a walkable test block. The actor owns both sites and preview skeletons. Place the Loan Site and Return Site on walkable ground, then adjust the child skeleton offsets and rotations for the front yards. The default sites are 28 m apart. Move the overlap zones onto the actual sidewalk and aim the world-space prompts toward the approach. Both the player capsule and each trigger need overlap events. In a World Partition test, keep this graybox actor loaded for the route. [Unreal actor attachment](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/BlueprintAPI/Transformation/AttachActorToComponent) · [Instanced static meshes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/Components/UInstancedStaticMeshComponent/AddInstance?application_version=5.5)

1. Visit the safety-vest skeleton first. The return should remain inactive.
2. Walk up to the bare skeleton. The hand truck should attach to the character and the loan label should point you toward the vest.
3. Walk to the vest in the same character. The truck should park and the return label should confirm success. An assigned courtyard gate should open.
4. Reenter both sites after delivery. The completed favor should remain complete.
5. Restart PIE, borrow the cart and destroy the borrower. The cart should return to the loan site so the respawned character can finish.

Use `OnFavorChanged` for a HUD objective and `CourtyardGate` for the optional shortcut. The first favor keeps its state for that level session. The standalone transition tests are automated. The editor smoke test verifies overlap events, prop attachment and the gate in the actual level.
